**A single-player 3D hockey simulation that runs in a browser tab.** Three formats — outdoor 11v11, Hockey5s and indoor — played on keyboard, touch or a standard gamepad. No account, no backend, no telemetry. Open the page and you are on the pitch.

This is **version 0.1, early access**: a real match loop with opponents, passing, shooting, keepers, restarts and results. Animation fidelity, officiating and team intelligence are still a good deal simpler than a commercial production, and there is no career mode or licensed roster.

## Before the menu, a match nobody is playing

The game opens by playing itself — a handful of shots in different formats, stadiums and weather, each one a real match the engine simulates from a seed, with no human on either side. Nothing is recorded, so the reel is different every time and adds nothing to the download. It keeps running behind the setup screen and stops the moment you take the field. Any tap, click or key skips it, and a browser asking for reduced motion never sees it.

## Pick a nation, any nation

All **133 FIH member associations** are in the flag grid, searchable and filtered by continental federation. Every nation carries a rating derived from the official FIH world ranking, and that strength is not decoration: it feeds duels, passing accuracy, decision speed and goalkeeping. When two nations would field similar shirts, the away side switches to its change strip.

Around that: formation, venue, weather, men's or women's squad, a 3, 6 or 10 minute match, and three difficulty levels. The interface speaks Dutch, English, German, French, Spanish and Polish, follows your browser on a first visit, and takes nation names from the browser's own region list.

## It knows the rules

Penalty corners are played out properly — stopper and striker at the top of the circle, runners and post players coming off the line, and seven routines to call. A rear tackle on someone bearing down on goal is a penalty stroke. Repeat offenders collect green and yellow cards and sit out two or five minutes on the scoreboard clock. Free hits inside the attacking 23 have to travel five metres before the circle.

The formats differ where the real ones do. Indoors and in Hockey5s a pass to a covered teammate goes off the side boards: the receiver is mirrored in the board, so you are aiming a cushion shot, and a dashed line shows the rebound. Indoors every shot is a push, and a defender may lay the stick flat on the floor — running through it is obstruction by the attacker.

Both sides play a shape rather than eleven individuals. The block slides with the ball, stretches in possession and squeezes out of it; one player presses, a second screens the next pass, but only once play crosses the press trigger, so nobody chases the ball into the far corner.

## Tournaments in the format the FIH plays them

A **World Cup** (16 nations, four pools, cross-overs into the quarter-finals), a **European Championship**, the **Olympic Games** and the **Pro League**, each with its own real structure down to the shoot-out point for a drawn Pro League match. Your nation joins the strongest others by ranking, the draw comes from a seed so it replays identically, and you play your own fixtures while the rest of the round is settled on ranking strength. Venues rotate, the weather is drawn per match day, and the tournament is saved on the device between sessions.

There is also a practice pitch with no clock: free play, penalty corner after penalty corner against a defence that runs out, or eight seconds one-on-one with the keeper from the 23.

## Under the shirt

The kit is cloth rather than paint — a colour map and a separate stretched bump map built from a photographed knit, a sheen that lifts where the jersey turns away from the camera, and a per-vertex weight that lets the hem flutter while the collar stays put, each shirt on its own phase so no two move in step.

The run is built from the joints out rather than from a loop: legs on gait curves scaled to the ground the stride has to cover, shoulders turning against the pelvis harder the faster you go, the free arm driving from a bent elbow, and the stick carried in the left hand with the blade swinging through the drive instead of being dragged along at a fixed angle.
