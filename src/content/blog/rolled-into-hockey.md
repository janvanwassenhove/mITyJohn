Nobody becomes a hockey coordinator on purpose.

My son started playing hockey. That was the entire plan: you bring a child to a
club, you stand beside a pitch, you drive home. Then my daughter started as well,
which doubled the driving and still looked like a plan. Then I was giving
training. Then I was coaching. Then I was a coordinator, and somewhere along the
way I had also written the software for
[the club's tenth birthday tournament](/blog/hockey-madness-celebrating-ten-years-with-chaos-and-charm/),
which was apparently not alarming enough for anyone to intervene.

I could not tell you at which point I agreed to any of this. Ask around at a
hockey club and you will find plenty of parents who can't either. It is less a
career path than an escalator, with a very friendly person at the top of it
asking whether you happen to be free.

## Finding the right explanation

Here is what the escalator does not mention. At some point you are standing in
front of a group, and you are expected to know things.

The knowledge exists. Hockey has no shortage of people who know exactly what they
are doing. But finding the right explanation, of this skill, for this age group,
at the moment you need it, is not always obvious. One explanation assumes you have
played since you were six. Another shows you the drill and not why it works. And
all of them speak a language in which a parent can nod for quite some time before
realising that a slap and flats are the same thing, and that a reverse is what the
person next to you has been calling a backhand.

So you do what parents do. You look things up the evening before, you ask someone
who has been on the escalator longer, and you hope the children ask nothing
difficult.

Children ask something difficult.

## A game can't teach your left hand

[HockeyEleven](https://hockeyeleven.com/) is a 3D hockey game that runs in a
browser: outdoor eleven-a-side, Hockey5s and indoor, on a keyboard, a phone or a
gamepad. Every FIH nation is in it, along with a few dozen clubs and a good many
evenings of my life. All the players and clubs are made up, and it has no ties to
the FIH, any association or any club.

A game, though, has an honest limit. Pressing a button teaches you the button.
You can score a magnificent reverse from the sofa and your left hand will have
learned nothing whatsoever.

That was the idea: tie the two together. Keep the game, and next to it build the
place where you learn the real thing, for real players, coaches and trainers, with
everything the game already has. The same players, the same pitches, the same
engine, but now it stands still when you ask, and turns around so you can see the
back of the movement. The Academy describes itself in one line: *real hockey to
learn, with the engine as board and as film*.

## A film you can walk around

Any skill opens as a film. You pick the camera, side, behind or close, you slow it
down to a half or a quarter, and you jump between the start, the load, the contact
and the follow-through. A filmed demonstration gives you the one angle the person
with the phone happened to be standing at. This one lets you go and stand
somewhere else.

## Tactics you can get wrong on request

Every coach has explained the flat triangle by drawing it in the air with a
finger. Here the game's own players stand on a real pitch, take up the right spot,
and then, on request, get it wrong. Too deep, and nobody is within reach of
anything. Too high, and the pass goes straight behind you with all that lovely
space to run into.

opinion. A defender standing in the wrong one and getting beaten is an argument.

## Something to do at the next training

Drills come as a board, in 2D or 3D, one step at a time, with coaching points, an
easier and a harder version, the equipment, and a print button. It also works on
a phone turned sideways, since that is what you tend to have with you beside a
pitch.

Sessions put those drills on a timeline. There are worked examples per age group
to start from, or you make your own, and then you print it or send it to the group
chat. The session also adds up what you need to bring. Eight cones, fifteen balls,
two goals, six bibs: that is the list you otherwise reconstruct in the car park.

Then winter comes, the club moves into the hall, and half of what you taught is
suddenly forbidden. No hit, no flats, no lift. The switch at the top of the Academy
turns all of it to indoor: its own techniques, starting with the block, and its own
drills and sessions. The outdoor skills say what happens to them indoors, usually in
the tone of someone breaking bad news.

And there is a glossary, with the English word you hear on the pitch next to the
Dutch one. The explanations are in Dutch for now, which tells you whose sideline
this started on.

## Where the game hands over

The part I like most is small. At the end of a quick match the game looks at your
own numbers, picks one thing, and offers to show it to you. Passes that did not
arrive, too few shots, too many shots conceded, or otherwise your first touch: one
tip, with a button that goes straight to the skill that helps.

<figure>

  <figcaption>A real full-time screen, from a match played by pressing pass and
  nothing else. It has a point.</figcaption>

It goes the other way as well. What gets built for the Academy finds its way back
into the match, such as the indoor block, the defensive walls and the backhand shot.

Neither of those will fix your push pass. That still takes a pitch, a ball and a
good many repetitions. But the distance between *that went badly* and *here is what it should
look like* is now one click, and it was a lot further than that when I started on
the escalator.

## Try it

The game is at **[hockeyeleven.com](https://hockeyeleven.com/)**: nothing to install
and no account needed for a match. The Academy is in beta and asks for a free account,
as do online play and the career. If you want to know how all this hangs together
underneath, the technical half of this story is
[Inside HockeyEleven](/blog/inside-hockeyeleven/).

And if somebody at your club has just asked whether you could maybe help out with
one session: I have seen where that goes. There is a game about it now.
